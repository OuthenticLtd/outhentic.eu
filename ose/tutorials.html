Tutorial 01 · Metronome

A polygon you can feel.

You're working on a 5/4 fill. The polygon at the centre of the metronome is a pentagon — five sides, one per beat. The bright dot orbits it, landing on each vertex precisely on the beat. Beat 1 is accented; you feel which side of the bar you're on at a glance.

The polygon morphs as you change the time signature. Drag BEATS up to 5 — pentagon. Up to 6 — hexagon.

SCENE 01 Open the Metronome from the home grid

The hero is the ClickTrackFace.

A 220–280dp tall amber-glass card with a 50sp light-weight monospace BPM digit. The eyebrow under it reads BPM · MODERATO — and updates live as you drag. Under 60: LARGO. Under 76: ADAGIO. Under 108: ANDANTE. Under 120: MODERATO. Under 156: ALLEGRO. Above: VIVACE, PRESTO, PRESTISSIMO.

SCENE 02 Tap beat numerals to cycle accents

Beat 1 accented. Beat 3 silent. Beat 5 ghosted.

Below the hero, a strip of beat numerals shows each beat. Tap any beat to cycle: silent → normal → accent. Try this in the demo above — tap any of the numbered dots. The active beat lights up while the click runs.

Pro tips

  • The polygon is the time signature. Triangle = 3, square = 4, pentagon = 5, hexagon = 6. Once you've got it, you can sight-read odd-meter pieces faster.
  • Tempo word lookup: under 60 = LARGO; 60–76 = ADAGIO; 76–108 = ANDANTE; 108–120 = MODERATO; 120–156 = ALLEGRO; 156–168 = VIVACE; 168–200 = PRESTO; above = PRESTISSIMO.
  • Bind the TAP button via MIDI Learn (long-press) so a foot pedal sets tempo on stage.
  • The metronome is also reachable from the Play Module via the metronome chip in the top bar — same engine, same presets.
Tutorial 02 · Practice Module

Slow down without changing pitch.

You found a riff on a video. The fingers fly, the notes blur. You drop the audio into Practice, slow it to 70%, and the pitch stays exactly where it was. Loop the hard bar, drill it, ramp it back up.

Phase-locked phase-vocoder under the hood. In OSE it operates on real audio at studio quality — here in the browser it's a visual demo of the decoupling.

SCENE 01 Open the take from the Library

Practice opens the take fullscreen.

The mini-visualiser at the top of the Practice library has an OPEN pill in mint. Tap it — or tap anywhere on the waveform thumbnail — and the editor opens. Same engine the Audio Module uses.

SCENE 02 Open SPEED & PITCH, drop a loop

The fold sits at the bottom of the editor.

Five icon-only fold tabs: EDIT · SPEED & PITCH · LOOPS · REPAIR · DSP. Tap the gauge icon for SPEED & PITCH. A semitone wheel, a cents slider and the speed wheel appear.

Then drag a region with the Select tool, promote it to a loop, and the loop wraps sample-perfect. Drill the bar, ramp the speed back up.

Pro tips

  • Speed and pitch are independent. Drop the speed to 70% AND transpose down a semitone if the song is in the wrong key for your voice.
  • Loop edges are sky-blue and grid-snap. Long-press the middle of the loop region to drag the whole thing, not just one edge.
  • Marker pins inside Practice carry over when you open the same take from Audio Module — the take library is shared.
  • Bind your foot pedal to the loop's start/stop via MIDI Learn (long-press the loop button).
Tutorial 03 · Tuner

The needle locks at ±3 cents.

Two minutes before downbeat. The bass is flat. The Tuner snaps green when you're inside ±3 cents — no twitching needle, no doubt. A 1-cent flutter doesn't pull the readout around.

Tuner 2 min · Beginner
Try it · Tuner snap-in-tune

Drag the slider. Watch the needle snap when you're close.

A4
+0¢

Slide the needle to ±2 cents — it snaps to centre and the green LED locks. Toggle Snap-in-tune off to see the raw drift the engine actually publishes.

Pro tips

  • The just-noticeable-difference for pitch is around 5–6 cents. The 3-cent snap band sits comfortably inside that, so the lock confirms you're there rather than fighting a forever-twitching reading.
  • The reference tone runs at the device's native output sample rate. Cheap converters resample at 44.1k or 48k regardless of source — OSE bypasses that so the reference stays exactly on pitch.
  • Tuning fork = percussive ADSR pluck. Speaker = continuous sine. Use the fork to match by ear, the speaker to hold a note for a full bar of tuning.
  • Settings cover Notation (English-Sharp / English-Flat / German / Solfège), Temperament (Equal / Just / Pythagorean), Transposition (±12 semitones).
Tutorial 04 · Play Module

Two voices, one keyboard.

Solo piano on the bottom half of the keyboard. Strings join from the split point up. Be the whole band — left hand bass, right hand pad, all from one preset. The keyboard is tinted in stripes so you can see exactly which voice you're triggering.

Play Module 5 min · Intermediate

Piano C4 — F4
Strings G4 — B5
G4

In OSE each layer can carry its own instrument source, key range, skin colour, microtonal scale and display name. Where ranges overlap, both voices play at once.

Pro tips

  • Multi-instrument zones can overlap. Set Layer 1's range to the full keyboard and Layer 2 only to the upper half — below the split you hear Layer 1; above, you hear both, layered.
  • Each zone has its own skin colour (12 user-selectable). Pick contrasting colours so your eyes can separate the zones at a glance on a busy keyboard.
  • Each zone can carry its own microtonal scale via the small scale micro-button on the right edge of the zone header. Useful for sitar over a Western lead.
  • Built-in OSE instruments are read-only — the save button forces "Save as New Preset". Your edit becomes its own thing in the User Presets folder.
  • Drum-pad mode is a layout toggle on the preset, not a separate module. The same multi-zone concept becomes 16 colour-coded pads.
Tutorial 05 · Live

Bind a foot pedal in two seconds.

You're playing live. The hands are full. You don't want to lift one off the keys to flip the loop on the second chorus. Long-press the loop button. The MIDI Learn sheet slides up. Tap your pedal. Done.

Live · system-wide 3 min · Beginner
Try it · Long-press for MIDI Learn

Hold the pad for 600ms. The sheet slides up.

LOOP TOGGLE
MIDI Learn · Listening
Press a key, turn a knob, tap a pedal

Hold the orange pad on the left. After 600ms a ring grows around it; the bottom sheet slides up and "listens" for your hardware. After ~1.6s OSE auto-binds (in this demo) and the pad confirms the binding.

In the real app you'd press a key on your MIDI keyboard or tap a foot pedal — the next CC, note or program-change message gets bound to the long-pressed control. Bindings are global and channel-aware.

Pro tips

  • Long-press is reserved app-wide for MIDI Learn. Any tappable control accepts it: knob, slider, pill, transport, the chevrons next to the instrument name, even toolbar verbs.
  • Bindings are global and channel-aware. The same CC on a different channel can hit a different control.
  • Most useful bindings for live: foot pedal → sustain (CC 64), foot pedal → loop start/stop, knob → filter cutoff, fader → expression, mod wheel → LFO depth.
  • Bluetooth MIDI keyboards work the same way. Pair via Settings → Bluetooth MIDI Devices.
  • Bindings persist between sessions. Set them once during soundcheck and they're there next gig.
